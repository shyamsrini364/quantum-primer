Start with a little wonder

A new lens.
A familiar world.

A leaf catches sunlight. A bird finds its way home. Inside a cell, energy changes hands. Ordinary moments can lead to extraordinary questions.

Quantum biology explores where the physics of very small things meets the workings of living systems. You don’t need to begin with equations. Begin with a question: how does life use light and move energy?

In Life on the Edge, Johnjoe McFadden and Jim Al-Khalili explore questions like these through photosynthesis, enzymes and bird navigation. Quantum Primer is a place to follow those ideas at your own pace—and see what different authors have to say.

01 / Light

More than
something we see.

What changes when we think of light as something living systems respond to? In Health and Light, John Ott starts with time-lapse observations of plants and follows his questions into the effects of different wavelengths.

Life on the Edge approaches light through photosynthesis: the process by which plants capture its energy. Together, the books offer different starting points for exploring light and life.

Health and Light, preface; Life on the Edge, chapter 4
02 / Water

A familiar substance.
Unfamiliar questions.

What happens where water meets the surfaces inside a living system? Catherine Clinton’s Optimize and R. D. Lee’s The Mitochondriac Manifesto discuss “exclusion zone,” or EZ, water: their accounts of ordered water near surfaces and its proposed role in energy.

These are the authors’ explanations. The interesting next step is to compare their passages, notice what they share, and separate an observation from a wider interpretation.

Optimize, chapter 2; The Mitochondriac Manifesto, PDF pages 122–124
03 / Energy & life

The world within
a living cell.

The library returns again and again to cells, energy and the environment. Jack Kruse’s Epi-paleo Rx connects his account of DHA and human biology to diet and surroundings. Lee explores mitochondria through his biophysical view of health.

In Your Spark is Light, Courtney Hunt discusses fertilization and the zinc spark, alongside broader interpretations of human creation. Read these perspectives as each author’s account, with the original passage close at hand.

Your Spark is Light, PDF pages 35–38; Epi-paleo Rx, PDF page 135
04 / Body clocks

Life has
a sense of time.

Light is more than something to see by. In Optimize, the daily pattern of light and darkness is also a timing signal. The book explores circadian rhythms: the recurring patterns that connect our waking and sleeping lives to the world outside.

Begin with a familiar contrast: a bright afternoon and a dark bedroom. Clinton uses changes in light and temperature to explain the cues the body receives. This is an entry into the book’s account of biological timing, rather than a reason to treat every light-related claim as a quantum effect.

Reading trail: Optimize, EPUB section 5
05 / Enzymes & tunnelling

A different way
through a barrier.

How do the tiny reactions that sustain life happen? Life on the Edge introduces enzymes, the molecular helpers that make reactions happen faster, then looks at the movement of electrons and protons.

Its account of quantum tunnelling offers a surprising picture: a particle can cross an energy barrier that it could not simply climb over. The authors connect this idea to electron transfer in biology and explore evidence for tunnelling in enzyme reactions. The scale matters—this is not a claim that whole cells pass through walls.

Reading trail: Life on the Edge, chapter 3, PDF pages 102–104
